Pasture Math

The pasture of 10 acres at 2000 lb of dry matter an acre, grazed at 50%, offers 10000 lb of grazable forage.

Boundary: arithmetic only Pasture Math counts dry matter with multiplication and division: the grazable forage a pasture offers, the dry matter a herd demands, the days the pasture holds, and the rest a rotation gives. It applies the counting as written, so it has not walked your fields and does not know your forage growth, your season, your species, your soil or your animals' condition, and it gives no verdict that a stocking is safe for the land or the herd. Every number here is your own entry.

Starting examples: 10 acres at 2000 lb an acre grazed at 50%, 5 head of 1000 lb eating 3% of body weight for 30 days, and a 5-paddock rotation at 6 days each.

The forage truth

The pasture of 10 acres at 2000 lb of dry matter an acre, grazed at 50%, offers 10000 lb of grazable forage.

The demand truth

The herd of 5 head at 1000 lb each, eating 3% of body weight a day for 30 days, demands 4500 lb of dry matter.

The days and rest truths

The pasture offering 10000 lb against a herd eating 150 lb a day holds for 66 days, and the rotation of 5 paddocks at 6 days of grazing each gives 24 days of rest before the return.
